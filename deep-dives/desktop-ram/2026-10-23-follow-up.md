# Desktop RAM follow-up — intermediate measurements, 2026-10-23

Final status: [bounded closeout](2026-10-23-bounded-result.md) completed after the user capped further work. The intermediate results below preserve the earlier failures and protocol changes.

Nine optimized runs completed: three repetitions each for 0, 100 and 1,000 notes, plus three new matched 1,000-note controls. Their source, driver, sampler, Electron/Node/QMD and model identities verified after measurement. All required applicable stages, complete semantic retrieval and observed descendant drain passed. The matched aggregates for [0/100 notes](2026-10-23-comparison-0-100.json) and [1,000 notes](2026-10-23-comparison-1000.json) include physical footprint, separate whole-stage RSS, active sampled peaks, CPU and latency ranges. Full acceptance remains pending at 10,000 notes; no numerical budget is adopted.

These smaller corpora do not demonstrate a whole-desktop RAM reduction. At 100 notes, home settled physical footprint ranged from 351–360 MB in the control and 353–362 MB in the optimized runs. Recovery ranged from 377–379 MB versus 373–380 MB. Both ranges overlap. Graph-exit footprint was higher in the optimized group: 421–433 MB versus 404–415 MB. Semantic-index active sampled peaks remained approximately 5.09–5.32 GB versus 5.25–5.32 GB. These are decimal units and ranges of three per-run statistics, not pooled sample ranges or absolute peaks.

The 100 MB total target remains unsupported. Even the optimized empty-corpus background stage measured 114–123 MB; its open home stage measured 334–349 MB. Model-active and open/closed UI modes need separate limits. Earlier process-only allocation improvements do not establish whole-desktop savings.

The suite then failed at the first 1,000-note edit: its old driver waited for an inserted marker that never appeared. That driver can admit an editable editor before the note is loaded, matching the previously identified readiness race. The canonical marker was absent and the sampler recorded an empty owned process tree after failure. This attempt is excluded. The six completed runs were retained only after independent artifact verification and selected-corpus report/comparison guards passed.

A new control and optimized 1,000-note pair completed with the same corrected loaded/visible-editor and explicit-deadline HTTP driver on both sides, plus the same gateway transport fix. Graph-exit settled footprint ranged from 540–542 MB in the new control and 513–524 MB in the optimized runs, a local reduction at that stage. Home ranges overlapped (355–369 MB versus 357–361 MB), recovery did not improve consistently (413–436 MB versus 422–486 MB), and optimized graph active peaks were higher (1,164–1,289 MB versus 1,121–1,174 MB). This does not establish a general whole-desktop reduction. Old and corrected driver results remain separate matched groups.

The next 10,000-note optimized attempt failed at the backend's 600-second semantic-index deadline, approximately 600.02 seconds after that stage began. Lexical work had completed; semantic completion/retrieval and subsequent lifecycle stages did not. The observed process tree drained completely. The second queue stopped and this attempt is excluded. Current matched 1,000-note semantic indexing took approximately 75–78 seconds on both sides; the older, differently driven 1,000-note controls took approximately 57 seconds. Machine state and protocol differences limit causal interpretation. Memory-pressure observations reached level 2 during model work.

A third queue subsequently completed a fresh 10,000-note control and optimized pair with identical benchmark-only deadlines: 900 seconds in the backend, 910 seconds in gateway forwarding and 950 seconds in the driver. [Protocol identities](2026-10-23-extended-deadline-protocol.json) record both original and modified source hashes, the identical gateway hash and the driver change. Only disposable frozen-source copies are changed. The default-deadline failure remains a failed gate, and this longer diagnostic pair cannot establish default-deadline acceptance. Production defaults are unchanged. This queue runs as a tracked foreground command so its completion/failure is surfaced by the command runner.

Limits remain substantial: this measures the frozen first-iteration runtime, with warm/uncontrolled filesystem caches, an unrelated installed app still running, and user/focus/GPU noise. External minute-level observations recorded AC power, pressure level 1 during the earlier 0/100-note runs and level 2 during later model work. Short tooling tests also ran outside the owned tree during early empty-corpus recovery. Three repetitions on one machine do not establish a universal bound or leak freedom. The soak, GPU trace and matched native-host experiments were explicitly deferred in the [closed bounded task](../../tasks/done/2026-10-23_tasks-desktop-ram-follow-up.md).
