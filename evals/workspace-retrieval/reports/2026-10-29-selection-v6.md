## What changed and what failed

Full-pool ordering replaces only the actual production first-forty stable partition with a stable partition over the already bounded grouped pool, retaining the same computed named subject and lexical/constraint/title/temporal/model guards. It adds no candidates, source-prefix reads or backend calls. **Copperfin and Mothlight orientation** now display their rank-42 introductions: intended purpose and named coordinator beside unchanged approved scope, exclusions and operating conditions. Both are substantive evidence gains on seen cases. There are no task or required-unit losses, but no fresh improvements either. This establishes the cutoff mechanism without meeting the frozen adoption threshold.

Rank-spread is an **evaluator-only** exploration policy: first two distinct expandable sources plus the final distinct expandable source, rather than first three. It cannot inspect gold, rich search or hidden source text. It loses six complete tasks and eleven required units across seven cases:

- Harbor/Juniper orientation lose explicit audience/service boundaries.
- Meridian/Vellum/Bracken/Quartz ranking lose the specific rehearsal-versus-live restriction and deviation-record requirement. Generic proposal disclaimers do not restore these conditions; the replacement read is unrelated Saffron material.
- The already-incomplete protected `two-stewards` case loses Patchbay's explicit purpose/steward binding when an unrelated Mosaic source replaces its read. This individual-unit loss would be hidden by comparing complete-task totals alone.

Separate source review checked every changed unit in all 18 case-arm rows and all twelve fresh cases across four arms. An independent provenance audit checked 75 unique packets, 333 excerpts and 177 expansions with zero range/hash errors. The two gains and all seven loss cases are substantive, rather than artifacts of stricter source-specific gold. Combined initial packets match ordering-only packets; its read losses match rank-spread. No policy, sources, expected clauses or thresholds were tuned after outputs.

## Read-only private-brain checks

Two subagents manually exercised the installed CLI on this machine: **14 strict read-only recalls and eight successful guarded expansions**, with two invalid overlong line ranges correctly rejected before corrected requests. Every recall used `--read-only`; there was no indexing, memory writing, remote access or model download. One installed/workspace comparison returned identical bytes. The first tester's before/after fingerprints covered 1,710 regular canonical/derived files and matched across bytes, size, mtime and ctime in both of its QA intervals. The second tester did not independently hash the tree. Private content and packets were not saved into repository/eval artifacts.

On three direct read-policy comparisons, spread helped one four-source query, made no change to a three-source query and exchanged useful context without a clear answer gain on a five-source query. Broad project orientation included adjacent and synthetic-test notes and did not establish current implementation. A historical quoted query preserved evidence versus import/writing dates and explicit bounded-discovery warnings. A targeted guarded read around a late-document excerpt restored context; line-one reads may miss later sections. These are a small manual usage sample, not exhaustive retrieval quality or answer judgments. Observed private recall/expansion timings under concurrent work are not performance acceptance evidence.
